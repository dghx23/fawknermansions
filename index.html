Fawkner Mansions

250 Punt Road, Prahran, Victoria · Est. 1910–11

Believed to be the oldest surviving residential flats in the City of Stonnington, and one of the oldest in Victoria.

Heritage Overlay HO448 Ernest H. Willis, 1910 Edwardian Free Classical

History

Few flats were built in Melbourne before the First World War — Fawkner Mansions is the only known surviving example from that period in the City of Stonnington.

Designed by architect Ernest H. Willis and constructed in 1910–11, Fawkner Mansions introduced a layout — flats arranged either side of a shared central stairwell — that would later become common across Melbourne's flat blocks. It stands today much as it was built, with later alterations assessed as not significant to its heritage value.

"Historically, Fawkner Mansions is the oldest residential flats in the City of Stonnington and one of the oldest in Victoria." — Victorian Heritage Database, Statement of Significance

Architecture

A bold, three-storey red-brick Edwardian Free Classical design on the corner of Punt and Commercial roads.

The building's massing is defined by parapeted corner towers and hip-roof entrance towers with semi-circular windows either side of the central entry. Finely varied brickwork — recessed spandrels, corbelled bands, rusticated pilasters — breaks up the mass and gives each level its own character, set behind an iron palisade fence on a bluestone plinth, a tessellated entrance path, and two mature Liquidambar trees.

A full photographic and 3D-model record of the building's exterior and common areas is planned as part of this project's digital preservation work — see the project's public repository for progress.

About David Hough

Academic researcher and writer, and current resident of Fawkner Mansions since 11 September 2026.

David Hough is a Melbourne-based academic researcher and writer with an international career spanning South Africa, Europe, and Australia. He holds a Master of Public Health from Macquarie University and a Bachelor of Business Systems from Bond University, with a professional background in assurance, governance, and evidence-based research built across major firms including PwC and Deloitte. That same international curiosity, and a research discipline honed across public health, risk, and policy, now turns toward Fawkner Mansions — a building whose century-long history had, until now, gone almost entirely undocumented.

David is also a co-founder of One1 Digital, a Sydney-based studio building custom software and automation — CRMs, workflow tools, branded client portals, and system integrations — for service-based organisations across education, healthcare, aged care, and community services. His sister now leads the company day to day; David's early contribution focused on strategy, risk, and business-development groundwork. Since returning to Melbourne, his current work runs through his own independent practice, Sentrix Digital, where RiskAtlas — a structured source-governance system for tracking legislative, regulatory, and policy material across jurisdictions — is the flagship project.

A consistent thread runs through all of it: a commitment to equity and access — making complex institutional information more transparent, navigable, and usable for the people it actually affects, whether that's someone navigating a benefits system, or a reader trying to understand the history of the building they live in. David brings that same advanced research methodology, rigorous source verification, and hands-on technical capability — from 3D capture to web development — to documenting, modelling, and preserving Fawkner Mansions for the long term.

About this project

This site is an independent research and heritage-documentation project about Fawkner Mansions, undertaken by a current resident of the building. It draws on the Victorian Heritage Database listing for Heritage Overlay HO448, alongside archival and on-the-ground research, with the aim of producing a book, a digital 3D/VR preservation record, and a short documentary.
