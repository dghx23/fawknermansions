# Ideas — Book, Documentary, Website, and the Pitch to Jeff Nguyen

## 1. Framing the pitch to Jeff Nguyen

- Lead with the heritage fact that does the most work: Fawkner Mansions is
  believed to be **the oldest surviving residential flats in the City of
  Stonnington, and one of the oldest in Victoria**. That is a genuinely rare
  claim and gives the building (and the owners) standing with heritage bodies,
  local media, and Stonnington Council — independent of any commercial value.
- Frame the ask narrowly and reversibly: a defined research period (e.g. 3
  months), specific deliverables (draft manuscript outline, 3D scan, short
  proof-of-concept documentary clip), and a defined cost to the owner (rent
  concession) — not an open-ended commitment.
- Offer the owners something concrete in return beyond "publicity": a
  professionally produced, permanent digital record of the building (3D
  model + book PDF) delivered to them regardless of whether the book is ever
  published commercially. That de-risks it for them even if the wider project
  stalls.
- Address the "might be demolished" angle carefully — as a preservation
  argument ("if anything ever happens to it, this exists"), not as a hint that
  demolition is expected or desired. Owners can be sensitive to anything that
  reads as devaluing the asset.
- Put a time limit and a checkpoint in the proposal itself (e.g. "after 6
  weeks I'll show you the draft chapter outline and 3D scan progress before
  the next 6 weeks proceed") — this is easier for an absent, hard-to-reach
  owner to say yes to than an open-ended 3-month commitment.
- Have a fallback ask ready if "free rent" is rejected: a smaller discount, or
  simply access permission (roof, common areas, archives, photography) with no
  rent change at all — the research and book can proceed either way.

## 2. Book structure and positioning (A5 hardcover)

- **Working structure:**
  1. Prologue — arriving at the building in 2026, the mystery of who else
     lives there, the present-day texture of the place.
  2. Ernest H. Willis and Edwardian Melbourne — the architect and the moment
     (1910–11) that produced the city's first purpose-built flats.
  3. Anatomy of the building — the architectural walkthrough (towers, brick
     detailing, the tessellated path, the Liquidambars), written for a general
     reader, illustrated with the 3D model renders.
  4. A century of residents — whatever can be traced through electoral rolls,
     Sands & McDougall directories, Trove newspaper archives, council rate
     records.
  5. The building today — present-day residents (as much as can be
     respectfully documented), the absent-ownership condition, what it's like
     to actually live there in 2026.
  6. Preservation — the case for the building's future, the 3D/VR record, and
     what "digital preservation" means for a heritage-listed building that
     isn't (yet) on the Victorian Heritage Register itself, only the local
     overlay.
  7. Appendix — full heritage citation, floor plans/3D model stills, timeline.
- A5 hardcover print-on-demand via **Amazon KDP** is workable for the trade
  edition; for the small heritage/university distribution run, consider a
  short-run offset or premium POD printer (e.g. via a local Melbourne printer)
  for better paper/binding quality where it will sit in a library or
  archive collection.
- Target recipients for the seeded copies: Stonnington History Centre / local
  library, State Library Victoria, Public Record Office Victoria, University
  of Melbourne and RMIT architecture/heritage faculties, National Trust
  Victoria, Prahran Mechanics' Institute.

## 3. Research sources worth chasing early

- **Trove** (newspapers, especially 1910–1911 building/tender notices, and any
  later incidents at the address).
- **Stonnington History Centre** local history collection.
- **PROV (Public Record Office Victoria)** — building permits, rate books.
- **Sands & McDougall Directories** (State Library Victoria) — year-by-year
  resident listings.
- **State Library Victoria picture collection** — for the 1960s-era photo's
  provenance and any earlier images than what's currently held.
- **Stonnington Council heritage/planning file for HO448** — may hold survey
  photos, past permit applications, and the original citation reports already
  referenced (Context P/L 2013; Nigel Lewis & Associates 1983).
- Land title history (Landata) for a documented chain of ownership, useful
  both for the book and for understanding who "the owner" legally is.

## 4. 3D model / VR preservation

- Capture method: photogrammetry (drone for exterior massing/roofline —
  check HO448 and any body-corporate/strata rules before flying; DJI or
  similar) plus a handheld/phone LiDAR or a rented terrestrial scanner
  (e.g. Matterport, or a mid-range LiDAR scanner) for interiors and common
  areas.
- Start with common areas and the exterior — the parts most likely to be
  accessible without disturbing other residents — before proposing interior
  flat scans as a stretch goal.
- Deliverables: a Matterport-style walkthrough (fast to produce, good for the
  website) as the near-term output, and a full photogrammetry mesh (Reality
  Capture / Metashape) as the long-term archival asset and VR source.
- VR target: a simple WebXR or Quest-compatible walkthrough is enough for a
  "future tours" pitch — no need to build a bespoke app initially.
- Archive the raw scan data (not just the processed model) — that's the part
  that actually has long-term preservation value if the building is ever
  altered.

## 5. Documentary (15–20 min)

- Structure mirrors the book: architect/era → building anatomy → present-day
  mystery → preservation case.
- Cheapest strong asset: the drone/photogrammetry footage doubles as
  documentary b-roll — plan the capture shoot to serve both outputs.
- Consider a short "day in the life of the building" observational sequence —
  visually interesting and sidesteps needing other residents to be interviewed
  on camera (privacy-sensitive given how little you know about them).
  Anything involving named identifiable current residents.
- If budget allows, one specialist interview (a Stonnington heritage officer,
  a local historian, or an architecture academic) gives the piece
  third-party credibility beyond "resident makes a video about own building."
- Distribution: YouTube (long-form) + a cut-down 60–90s vertical version for
  Instagram/TikTok to drive traffic to the book and website.

## 6. Website

- Keep it separate in scope from the existing `wifi-portal/` captive-portal
  project in this repo — the WiFi splash page is a guest-facing utility, the
  history website is a public-facing heritage/marketing site. They can share
  the same visual language (oxblood brick, bluestone, cream render, black
  iron, the `FM` monogram) established in `wifi-portal/GROK_CODE_PROMPT.md`.
- Suggested sections: History, Architecture (with 3D/VR embed), Gallery
  (real photos only — clearly dated and captioned), The Book, Contact/Press.
- SEO/heritage-interest angle: Stonnington's HO448 listing and "oldest
  surviving flats" claim are strong, specific search terms worth targeting.

## 7. Monetisation / sustainability

- Book sales (KDP royalties) are unlikely to be large on their own; treat them
  as reputational/heritage-interest income, not the primary return.
- Documentary ad revenue / sponsorship from a heritage-adjacent brand
  (architecture practice, heritage insurer, local council tourism body).
- Potential paid VR tour licensing to Stonnington Council, National Trust, or
  a local tourism body once the model exists.
- Speaking/writing spin-off: local history talks, an article pitch to
  Melbourne heritage or architecture publications while the book is in
  progress, to build an audience ahead of launch.
