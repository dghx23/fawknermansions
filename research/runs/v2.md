## Verified / high-confidence findings

### Identity, architect and heritage status
- Current Stonnington Planning Scheme identifies **Fawkner Mansions, 250 Punt Road, Prahran** as **HO448**. The schedule applies tree controls to two Liquidambars and identifies the iron palisade fence; it explicitly says the place is **not included on the Victorian Heritage Register**.
- The Australian Institute of Architects Victorian register lists **1910**, multi-residential, architect **Ernest H. Willis**, 250 Punt Road, Prahran, HO448.

### The “oldest flats” claim
City of Port Phillip heritage comparative analysis states:
- Melbourne Mansions in Collins Street (1906, demolished in the 1950s) were the first purpose-built flats in Victoria;
- Fawkner Mansions, built in 1910, was the first purpose-built flats outside the city and is now the **oldest surviving block of flats in Melbourne**.

**Publication-safe wording:** “Built around 1910, Fawkner Mansions is widely recognised in Victorian municipal heritage analysis as Melbourne’s oldest surviving purpose-built block of flats; Melbourne Mansions (1906) preceded it but was demolished.”

Do not state “Australia’s first apartment building” or “Australia’s oldest apartment building” as settled fact.

### Date contradiction remains real
- AIA: **1910**.
- South Yarra Heritage Review: **1909–10**, citing Sawyer 1982.
- Stonnington Thematic Environmental History: **1912**, built for George Fairbairn Jr.
- Existing project copy uses **1910–11**.

**Current control:** use “c.1910” or “1909–12, most commonly dated 1910” in research writing until the original permit, contract, rate book or contemporary construction notice resolves it.

### Alfred Hospital / nurses
Victorian Auditor-General reporting provides unusually strong primary evidence:
- Fawkner Mansions accommodated **80 nursing staff**.
- It closed in **October 1987**.
- It was operating at an annual deficit of about **$90,000**.
- It was valued at about **$1.2 million in September 1989**.
- 2½ years after closure the hospital still had not determined its future use.

The Age/Commercial Real Estate report says Alfred Hospital purchased the building in **1950**, attributing that detail to a Stonnington heritage citation. This purchase date is credible but should ideally be corroborated through title/hospital records.

### 2013 closure and homelessness response
HomeGround’s 2013 annual report says Fawkner Mansions gave residents **six weeks’ notice** and that HomeGround coordinated the rehousing of **45 residents** after the Prahran rooming house closed.

### 2016 photographs / 2019 article — important correction
The widely circulated “mystery building” story is a **The Age article published 16 April 2019** (Benjamin Preiss; republished by Commercial Real Estate). Several embedded photographs carry file captions dated **19 August 2016**. This likely explains later references to a “2016 Age article”.

The 2019 article reports:
- late-1990s onward use as accommodation for people facing homelessness;
- co-owner **Jeffrey Nguyen** saying deterioration and damage forced closure;
- more than 70 rooms;
- owner-funded gradual renovation;
- finance difficulty attributed by Nguyen to the combination of heritage obligations and the Punt Road acquisition overlay;
- housing-sector interest in restoration of low-cost accommodation.

### Punt Road Public Acquisition Overlay
Official/review material places the road-widening reservation in the **original 1954 planning scheme**. A 2024 ABC report describes the current overlay as about 20 metres wide, 2.6 km long and affecting roughly 140 properties. This corrects media wording that said the overlay had existed “since the 1960s”.

### Current hotel operation
Booking.com currently lists Fawkner Mansions Heritage Hotel at 250 Punt Road. Review data is volatile:
- the user-supplied audit captured **5.4/10 from 384 reviews**;
- a later Booking.com review page surfaced **409 verified reviews**;
- Tripadvisor currently shows **1.0/5 from only one review**.

These must be presented as dated snapshots, not enduring facts.

## New revelations / contradictions identified in v2

### 1. The supplied “drug den / brothel / chronic squalor” claim is not verified
The user-supplied audit attributes these phrases to a supposed **2016 The Age** article. In this run, the located Age/Commercial Real Estate article is from **2019**, with 2016 photographs, and the searched copy does **not** support those phrases.

**Action:** quarantine this claim. Do not publish it as historical fact unless the original article containing those exact assertions is located and checked.

### 2. The likely source of the 2016/2019 confusion is now identifiable
The Age article is 2019, but image captions are dated August 2016. This is a provenance issue rather than proof of a separate 2016 article.

### 3. The PAO date can be corrected
Use **1954**, not “since the 1960s”.

### 4. Review-platform divergence is less meaningful than the supplied audit implies
Tripadvisor’s apparent 1.0 score rests on only **one review**. It should not be compared as though it has similar statistical weight to Booking.com’s hundreds of reviews.

### 5. “First purpose-built flats” needs geographic precision
Domain says Melbourne did not see its first purpose-built block until 1910, while Port Phillip’s heritage review explicitly identifies Melbourne Mansions (1906) as earlier. Prefer the official heritage comparative analysis.

### 6. Original configuration remains unresolved
Storey of Melbourne says 25 five-room “complete suites”; Domain says 27 small flats with shared bathrooms/kitchens. Neither should be treated as settled until original plans or early advertisements are found.

### 7. State heritage-registration history remains incomplete
A 1993 library catalogue record confirms a “Submission in relation to the proposed inclusion on the historic buildings register” for Fawkner Mansions. Current HO448 says it is not on the VHR. The reason/outcome of that 1993 process still requires archival retrieval.

## Corrections to earlier/project wording
- Replace “heritage-listed” where ambiguity could imply state registration with **“protected under Stonnington Heritage Overlay HO448”**.
- Replace categorical “built 1910–11” with **“c.1910”** in public-facing copy unless a source is cited for the narrower range.
- Replace “oldest residential flats in Stonnington and one of the oldest in Victoria” with the exact wording of the chosen official citation where possible.
- Do not repeat “drug den”, “brothel” or “chronic squalor” without locating the underlying original source.
- Do not present Tripadvisor 1.0 as a meaningful platform-wide consensus; disclose the one-review denominator.
- Do not infer that present cleanliness scores are caused by the building’s past.
