Core project

Preserve the building, verify its history, make the record useful

The heritage workstream is the original project: establish a defensible historical record for Fawkner Mansions, digitally preserve the building, and turn that work into a book, documentary, website and reusable archive.

Project briefVersioned researchSource library

Deliverables

Verified historical record

Chronology, people, ownership/use, architecture and planning history, with claims traceable to primary or strongest available sources.

Book

Hardcover/local-history publication drawing on the verified record, archival material, oral histories and present-day documentation.

3D / VR preservation

Photogrammetry and digital-twin capture of the exterior and agreed common areas, preserving a dated record independent of future physical change.

Documentary & web

Short history documentary plus a public companion site designed to make the building's story accessible beyond the physical property.

Opportunity / risk balance

Opportunity

A rare combination of a surviving early apartment building, a living accommodation business and a largely unwritten social history creates a distinctive heritage story.

Owner value

Permanent digital record, improved public narrative, usable heritage assets and potential media/academic relationships.

Credibility risk

Inflated “first/oldest” claims, uncertain dates, circular web sourcing and uncleared imagery could undermine the project if not controlled.

Control

Keep versioned research runs, source hierarchy, contradiction log, provenance manifest and publication-safe wording.

Cross-reference: The detailed evidence, contradictions and project-wide research risks live in Research & Development. The separate accommodation systems are intentionally kept out of the heritage narrative except where they intersect with the building's contemporary history.
